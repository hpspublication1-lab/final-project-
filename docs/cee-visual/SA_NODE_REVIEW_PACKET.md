## What the sources say

Sources checked on 27 Nov 2026, paraphrased (open the links for the original wording):

| Source | Location given |
|---|---|
| StatPearls, *Physiology, Sinoatrial Node* (NCBI Bookshelf NBK459238) | Where the crista terminalis in the upper right atrial wall meets the opening of the superior vena cava; a crescent-shaped cluster a few square millimetres in area |
| Anderson, Ho & Anderson, *Location and vascular supply of sinus node in human heart*, Br Heart J 1979;41:28–32 (PMC459576) | Beneath the epicardium, in the terminal sulcus; because the blood supply varies, the whole superior cavo-atrial junction should be treated as a danger area in surgery |
| Infant hearts studied by reconstruction (Br Heart J; PMC514694) | On the **lateral** side of the superior cavo-atrial junction in 22 of 25 hearts |
| Sánchez-Quintana et al., *Sinus node revisited…*, Heart 2005;91:189–194 (PMC1768731) | Crescent-shaped, about 13.5 mm long, no fibrous sheath; wholly subepicardial in 72 % of hearts, partly subendocardial in the rest |
| Kalyanasundaram et al., *3D functional anatomy of human sinoatrial node*, 2023 (PMC9897959) | A single elongated structure within the wall at the superior vena cava / intercaval junction, bordering the crista terminalis |
| Elsevier Complete Anatomy, right atrium entry | The upper end of the sulcus terminalis is the external landmark for the node |

**For the reviewer's convenience, not a conclusion:** every source places it in the right atrium,
at its junction with the superior vena cava, along the terminal sulcus / crista terminalis, usually
just beneath the epicardium, and the studies that give a side say **lateral**. "Anterolateral" and
"posterolateral" are both looser descriptions, and none of the sources above uses either as the
defining location. At entrance-exam level, the quiz's answer (a) matches all of them.
